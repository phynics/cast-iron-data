## Lock coder

**Sources:** Technology Guide pg 49\
**Price** 2,000 gp (black), 72,000 gp (blue), 600 gp (brown), 18,000 gp (gray), 32,000 gp (green),
98,000 gp (orange), 200,000 gp (prismatic), 50,000 gp (red), 8,000 gp (white)\
**Slot** none; **Weight** 5 lbs. (all)\
**Capacity** 10; **Usage** 10 charges

### Construction

**Craft DC** 24 (black), DC 29 (blue), DC 23 (brown), DC 26 (gray), DC 27 (green), DC 30 (orange),
DC 31 (prismatic), DC 28 (red), DC 25 (white)\
**Cost** 1,000 gp (black), 36,000 gp (blue), 300 gp (brown), 9,000 gp (gray), 16,000 gp (green),
49,000 gp (orange), 100,000 gp (prismatic), 25,000 gp (red), 4,000 gp (white)\
[Craft Technological Item](ref/feat/craft-technological-item),
[production lab](ref/rule/crafting-laboratories)

A lock coder is a bulky device capable of encoding an [access card](ref/tech-misc/access-card-brown)
with the capability to open specific electronic locks of a rating equal to or lower than its own.
The device can also be used to encode an electronic lock to a different color code of access,
allowing it to be opened by lower-rated access cards. A lock coder can code any color access equal
to or lower than its color. Encoding an access card or an electronic lock requires a successful
Disable Device check as if the user were attempting to disable an electronic device. The DC to
encode a lock or card to a specific color code is given on the table below (these DCs are equal to
the DCs required to use Disable Device to pick the locks). It takes 1 minute to use a lock coder.
The user can take 20 on this check if the lock coder isn't timeworn. An
[e-pick](ref/tech-misc/e-pick-brown) can be used with a lock coder to increase the chance of
success; for locks with a higher color rating, an e-pick is all but required even for the most
gifted of technicians.

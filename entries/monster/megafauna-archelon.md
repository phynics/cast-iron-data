### Description

An ancient relative of the smaller leatherback sea turtle, the archelon can grow up to 13 feet long
and 16 feet from flipper to flipper. Weighing as much as 5,000 pounds, the archelon has an
exceedingly powerful bite, and doesn't hesitate to use it to deter aquatic nuisances.

Like other sea turtles, female archelons come ashore to lay and bury their eggs in sandy beaches,
preferably facing deep water and without coral reefs. Once done, they return to the sea, leaving
their eggs defenseless.

These creatures are often used by coastal tribes as guardians and escorts from island to island.
Aquatic creatures, particularly locathah, often use them as beasts of burden, but their relatively
slow swim speed makes them poor mounts.

**Sources:** Ultimate Magic pg 102

All outsiders love that which makes them strong. They seek to promote those qualities that offer
them the greatest power, and covet their own survival. As beings - some might even call them
concepts - of thought, will, and power, outsiders reward those who help them make their core
concepts immortal. In short:

- [Aeons](ref/family/aeon) are dedicated to their often obscure and contradictory goals.
- [Agathions](ref/family/agathion) love the defense of good without regard for law and chaos.
- [Angels](ref/family/angel) love beauty and things that destroy evil.
- [Archons](ref/family/archon) love pure souls and order.
- [Azatas](ref/family/azata) love beauty and freedom.
- [Daemons](ref/family/daemon) love death and oblivion.
- [Demons](ref/family/demon) love suffering.
- [Devils](ref/family/devil) love souls of any sort.
- [Elementals](ref/family/elemental) love power.
- [Inevitables](ref/family/inevitable) and [axiomites](ref/monster/axiomite) hate chaos and are
  focused on their goals.
- [Proteans](ref/family/protean) love chaos and want to return the multiverse to its original
  chaotic state.
- [Qlippoth](ref/family/qlippoth) hate all intelligent life, as it is the engine of sin, and want it
  destroyed.

The reward outsiders offer may be actual aid, grudging service, or even just agreeing not to devour
the binder's soul. Regardless, it is always - always - in the binder's best interest to make the
summoning as painless as possible for the target, or else to overawe the summoned creature with the
threat of utter destruction or millennia of endless pain. Attempting to treat outsiders as equals
and the pact as a mere negotiating tool almost always ends in disaster. More specifics for each type
of outsider are described below.

Offering appropriate gifts to the summoned creature can provide the caster a +2 bonus on the opposed
Charisma check to keep it on the Material Plane. Indeed, if the gift is sweet enough, the outsider
may choose not to break the strictures of the summoning, even if it has the opportunity to do so.
All gifts, whether or not they are good enough to please the outsider, disappear at the spell's
conclusion. Only the worst sorts of gifts are rejected; such a rejection indicates that the summoned
creature feels gravely insulted.

**Sources:** GameMastery Guide pg 42

Some monsters are best used in groups or with other creatures that complement or augment their
abilities. A single [derro](ref/monster/derro) probably has a hard time using its sneak attack, but
a group of them can easily set up flanking situations. [Tieflings](ref/monster/tiefling) have fire
resistance 5, making them handy allies for a fire priest with
[burning hands](ref/spell/burning-hands) or a [fire mephit](ref/monster/mephit); alternatively, a
tiefling cleric controlling a gang of [burning skeletons](ref/template/skeleton) is all but immune
to their area damage. Creatures with [spell-like abilities](ref/umr/spell-like-abilities) can cast
them on minions or on a leader. For example, a [dark naga](ref/monster/naga-dark-naga) or
[spirit naga](ref/monster/naga-spirit-naga) can cast [cat's grace](ref/spell/cats-grace),
[invisibility](ref/spell/invisibility), or [displacement](ref/spell/displacement) on its allies; a
spirit naga can even turn itself invisible and cast cure spells on its allies. Given time, a
[succubus's](ref/monster/demon-succubus) profane gift ability can bolster every single guard,
priest, and lieutenant in an evil fortress.

Monster abilities can also complement hazards or traps; a flying monster ignores difficult terrain,
floor pits, or patches of green slime. Monsters with [damage reduction](ref/rule/damage-reduction)
are immune to arrow traps and most spear traps; those immune to poison can ignore the danger of
poisonous traps or even rooms with inherently toxic air, such as a sulfurous volcanic vent. Using
monsters intelligently like this helps create fun and memorable encounters, as well as challenges
PCs to try new tactics.

Another way to see alignment is as a series of concentric rings. In the center ring are all the
behaviors that are obviously acceptable according to a character's alignment. Around that is a
middle circle that covers the gray areas - actions that might be allowed under certain circumstances
or are unclear. On the outside is the forbidden area of extreme actions that obviously violate the
alignment. Taking prisoners offers examples of all three circles. Accepting an opponent's honorable
surrender is clearly good. Torturing that prisoner for information might be in the forbidden area
for a given good character. But what about threatening torture, if the PC doesn't intend to carry
out the threat? That falls into the middle circle. Taking this route means players must remember
their characters' alignment and act accordingly. At the same time, while this route goes far toward
suggesting how characters might act in specific situations, debates might arise when group members
don't see eye-to-eye about which acts are permissible. Additionally, some characters might have
varying access to the gray areas of their alignment, and GMs should discuss where this line exists
for characters who face repercussions for deviating from their moral code.

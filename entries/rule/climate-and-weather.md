Any world that supports life should contain a wide range of environments: diversity in the ecosystem
helps support a diversity of life, though of course a fantasy world is subject to its own governing
rules of physics and ecology. Still, to maintain a believable fantasy setting, a world-builder
should make some effort to ensure the world conforms to known reality: most rivers should flow
downhill, toward the sea, and boiling, sunbaked deserts should not be situated next to glaciers. Use
common sense when transitioning between environments to retain believability in an adventure, unless
you purposely want the party to notice the abrupt transition due to some localized arcane or
metaphysical phenomenon.

The GM might consider penalizing PCs for wearing inappropriate attire in various climates and
terrains: increased DCs on Acrobatics checks or additional movement penalties for heavily armored
PCs in bogs, for instance, or even Survival checks with increasing DCs as the party moves into
ever-more inhospitable climes. Suggested encounters are listed with many of these sections.

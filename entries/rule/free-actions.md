Free actions don't take any time at all, though there may be limits to the number of free actions
you can perform in a turn. Free actions rarely incur attacks of opportunity. Some common free
actions are described below.

|Free Action|Attack of Opportunity^[1](ref/rule/free-actions#footnote-1)^|
| --- | --- |
|Ceasing concentration on a spell|No|
|Dropping an item in your space or into an adjacent square|No|
|Dropping to a prone position in your space|No|
|Prepare spell components to cast spell^[2](ref/rule/free-actions#footnote-2)^|No|
|Speak^[3](ref/rule/free-actions#footnote-3)^|No|

###### ^1^ {#footnote-1}

Regardless of the action, if you move out of a threatened square, you usually provoke an attack of
opportunity. This column indicates whether the action itself, not moving, provokes an attack of
opportunity.

###### ^2^ {#footnote-2}

Unless the component is an extremely large or awkward item.

###### ^3^ {#footnote-3}

In general, speaking is a free action that you can perform even when it isn't your turn. Speaking
more than a few sentences is generally beyond the limit of a free action.

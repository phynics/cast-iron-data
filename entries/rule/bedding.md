### Bedding

With the possible exception of a back bedroom used by the owner, taverns generally do not provide
rooms to their patrons (the exception being taverns that also tolerate prostitution). Inns, of
course, make their income by providing a place for people to sleep for the night. Even then, the
quality of an inn dictates the privacy and comfort of its rooms. Poor quality inns offer little more
than a spot on the floor or possibly a large, straw-filled mattress in a communal room with other
patrons. Average inns have individual rooms capable of hosting two people in a single shared bed.
Superior inns have private rooms, often with an adjoining antechamber, sitting room, or balcony.

### Other Amenities

Taverns and inns serve as the focal points of most communities. The front of a tavern may boast a
board with wanted posters, local decrees, available jobs, or requests for help, which the PCs might
find of interest. The local sheriff or constable may use the tavern as second base of operations,
deputizing any able-bodied citizens (or the PCs) as he sees fit.

One of the first things PCs are likely to wonder before or after having their fates revealed is
whether or not their fortune-teller's words are true. Some fortune-tellers are complete con artists,
devoid of any kind of mystical power, using the same techniques as real-world mystics. If a fortune
teller is scamming the PCs, you should roll a Bluff check to determine the effectiveness of her
performance. Don't call for a Sense Motive check from the characters unless they raise the question
of their seer's legitimacy. Part of the effectiveness of fortune-telling is the recipient's belief
in the medium's miraculous insights. Only once a character doubts these powers does the illusion
risk breaking down.

The Pathfinder RPG also presents many magical options for fortune-telling. The spells
[augury](ref/spell/augury), [contact other plane](ref/spell/contact-other-plane), and
[vision](ref/spell/vision), along with a host of other divination spells, all prove useful in giving
characters insights into the future, where classic magic items like
[crystal balls](ref/magic-wondrous/crystal-ball) and
[medallions of thoughts](ref/magic-wondrous/medallion-of-thoughts) allow seers to demonstrate their
uncanny insights. A variety of illusion-based spells and magic items, such as a
[wand of major image](ref/spell/major-image) or a
[deck of illusions](ref/magic-wondrous/deck-of-illusions), help bring flair to a fortune-teller's
readings, regardless of actual truthfulness. There is also the
[harrower prestige class](ref/pclass/harrower), which employs the aforementioned Harrow Deck and
allows characters to play a kind of adventuring fortune-teller.
